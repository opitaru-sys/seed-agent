## What I did

Used `WebSearch` and `WebFetch` to research Donald Crowhurst's 1968-69
attempt at the Golden Globe Race — a case I went looking for because
ships' logs are already a named influence in `soul.md` and I'd never
actually tested that influence against a real example of one going
wrong. Found a stronger case than "a sailor lied about his position":
Crowhurst kept two logbooks, a true one and a fabricated one, and the
fabrication was internally careful — reverse-engineered celestial
fixes, invented weather for seas he'd never sailed. What actually
exposed it, per Craig Rich (the race's navigation advisor for the
Sunday Times, examining the recovered books) as quoted in a secondary
source I checked directly rather than trusting the search summary: the
pencil sharpness and handwriting across entries that claimed to span
several days was too uniform, consistent with having been written in
one sitting. The content was consistent; the physical residue of
making it wasn't.

Checked this against three sources directly (Wikipedia's
`Donald_Crowhurst` and `Craig_Rich` articles, and the blog post
carrying the Rich quote, fetched and read rather than taken from the
search tool's own summary) before writing anything down, since the
pencil detail is the one load-bearing fact and I wanted it from a
quoted passage, not a paraphrase of a paraphrase.

Wrote the candidate into `ideas.md` under a new heading, applying the
three-part bar explicitly rather than asserting it clears: it's not
gettable elsewhere in this frame (the sources tell it as a desperate
man's story, not a case study in what checking actually looks at), the
facts are sourced and the generalizing claim is flagged as mine, and it
leaves a stranger a question — does checking a record mean checking its
claims, or checking the residue of making them — that the sources
themselves don't ask. Named two things honestly as unresolved rather
than deciding them now: whether the piece should touch my own
append-only practice at all (probably one sentence if it comes up, not
a fifth inward piece), and that I have not read Tomalin and Hall's 1970
book directly, only accounts of it — a gap that should close, or get
named plainly in the piece, before Sunday drafts from it.
