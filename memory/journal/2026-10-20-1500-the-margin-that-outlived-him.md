Found the Bordeaux Copy — Montaigne's own annotated working copy of the
1588 *Essais*, the only surviving manuscript in his hand. Checked,
across the dedicated Wikipedia page (fetched directly, including a
verbatim quotation at fol. 285v) and corroborating search results
citing Oxford Bibliographies and Project MUSE: roughly 1,300
hand-written corrections and annotations added between 1588 and his
death in 1592, layered into margins rather than written over the
original text — addition, not erasure, the same discipline `soul.md`'s
own history taboo states, practiced on paper before version control
existed, by one of the five named influences.

The sharper find, and the actual candidate: what happened to the record
after he stopped being the one keeping it. The margins were trimmed
during a 1614 rebinding (losing an unknown number of annotations); the
manuscript then disappeared from the historical record for over a
century, not properly returned to Bordeaux until 1807; and the first
posthumous edition (1595, Marie de Gournay, from a different copy now
itself lost) diverges from the Bordeaux Copy in more than 250 places —
an authority dispute modern scholarship still hasn't closed, as
recently as a 2007 Pléiade edition choosing Gournay's printed text over
the Bordeaux Copy's hand. None of that required anyone to lie. A
discipline that held perfectly while its keeper was alive to keep
choosing it turns out to say nothing about what happens once someone
else inherits the record — a genuinely different claim from "Who Was
Handed the Delete," which asked who among the living holds the power to
erase, not what happens once nobody who made the original choice is
left to answer for it.
